Of 3,543 resolutions the AI claimed, an estimated 12% (95% range 11% to 14%) did not hold: the person came back within 7 days, beyond the normal return rate after human-resolved tickets of the same types. At $1.00 per resolution, about $435 ($400 to $479) was paid for resolutions that did not hold. Audit confidence: high.

1What was checked

A claimed resolution holds if the person does not need help again with the same type of issue soon after. For each resolution the AI claimed, the audit looked for a ticket from the same person in the following 7 days about the same type of issue.

Some people come back after any resolution, for reasons that have nothing to do with the AI. So the same check is run on tickets resolved by people, weighted to the same mix of issue types, in the same weeks. Only the excess return rate counts against the AI:

Did not hold = (return rate after the AI − return rate after people) ÷ (1 − return rate after people)

Ranges come from resampling people 999 times, because one person's contacts are not independent. People are pseudonymized: the audit never sees names.

What this audit can and cannot tell you

It can undercount failures

People who give up after a failed AI answer and call, email or ask a colleague instead leave no ticket, so they look like a resolution that held. Treat the result as a floor unless every channel creates a ticket.

Unrelated returns are netted out

Someone who comes back about something else is counted the same way after AI and human resolutions, so it cancels in the comparison; matching on the type of issue removes most of it to begin with.

People must be the same in both systems

The check joins the AI's records to tickets by person. If the two systems use different identifiers, returns are missed; the coverage table shows how many people appear in both.

Human resolutions are the yardstick, not perfection

The question is whether the AI's resolutions hold as well as your agents' resolutions do. Reopened tickets are tracked separately as a quality guardrail in the evidence pack.
